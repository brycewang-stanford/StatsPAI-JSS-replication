\section{Computational performance}\label{sec:performance}

The timing study covers four estimators: high-dimensional fixed effects
(\code{sp.fast.feols}), Callaway--Sant'Anna staggered DiD
(\code{sp.callaway\_santanna}), classical synthetic control
(\code{sp.synth(method="classic")}), and double machine learning
(\code{sp.dml(model="plr")}). It asks whether these entry points are slower than their references on
routine work, on identical inputs and tasks. It is not a claim of general speed
superiority.

\subsection{Benchmark protocol}

A speed ratio compares two implementations only if both perform the same
task on the same input. Every input is drawn once, in \proglang{Python},
written as CSV at round-trip precision, and read by \emph{both} sides. Each
side records the SHA-256 of the file it read, and the comparator refuses a
row whose two sides read different data or come from different runs. Outside the timed call each side records its target
quantity, so the table can show that the two solved the same problem. Both
sides run on one thread (\pkg{fixest}, \pkg{data.table}
\citep{barrett2026datatable}, BLAS, and \pkg{Numba} pinned), on one machine
(Apple Silicon arm64, 8 physical cores, 24~GB, macOS~26), with \statspai{}
1.40.2 and \pkg{did} 2.5.1. Times are medians over repetitions after a
warmup, with their interquartile range, on an otherwise idle machine; the
archive records the load and the protocol.

% Keep the four task descriptions on one page.
\Needspace*{16\baselineskip}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.25em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item[HDFE.] OLS of \(y\) on two regressors with firm and year effects
absorbed and iid standard errors, \(N \in \{10^4,10^5,10^6\}\):
\code{sp.fast.feols} against \pkg{fixest::feols}~\citep{berge2018efficient}.
\item[CS-DiD.] A balanced staggered panel of five periods with cohorts
first treated in periods 2, 3, and 4 (the same calendar on both sides),
\(N_{\mathrm{units}} \in \{1{,}000,5{,}000,25{,}000\}\): group-time ATTs
by outcome regression against the never-treated, analytic standard
errors (no multiplier bootstrap or uniform band on either side), the
joint pre-test, and the simple and dynamic aggregations.
\code{sp.callaway\_santanna} returns all four in one call; the reference
runs \pkg{did::att\_gt} and two \pkg{did::aggte} calls.
\item[SCM.] A two-factor, 30-period panel with
\(n_{\mathrm{donors}} \in \{20,50,100\}\) and the specification of
\citet{abadie2010synthetic}: one outcome special predictor per
pre-treatment year and a nested search over \(V\), weights and gap only,
no placebos, for \code{sp.synth(method="classic")} and
\pkg{Synth::synth}. The two solvers differ by design (below). The reference
call sets \code{optimxmethod = "BFGS"}, while that package's default also runs
Nelder--Mead. The package's \emph{default} call is a different task and is
reported separately.
\item[DML.] Partially linear DML with linear nuisance learners and five
folds, public-workflow construction included, \(N \in
\{1{,}000,5{,}000,10{,}000\}\): \code{sp.dml(model="plr")} against
\pkg{DoubleML} Python in the same process, so that language-runtime
overhead does not dominate a comparison of milliseconds.
\end{description}

\subsection{Measured performance results}

Table~\ref{tab:track-c-perf} reports the largest size of each benchmark
and Figure~\ref{fig:track-c-loglog} all measured sizes. In every row the
two sides agree on the target quantity. They agree to \(2\times10^{-14}\)
for the HDFE coefficient and to \(4\times10^{-13}\) for the simple ATT.
For the SCM gap they agree to at most \(5\times10^{-5}\) across sizes, with two non-convex
solvers landing on the same solution. For DML, whose folds coincide under
the shared seed, they agree to \(4\times10^{-15}\) standard errors.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.25em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item \textbf{HDFE.} \statspai{} is faster at every size, by
\(6.1\times\) at \(N=10^4\) (0.0007 against 0.0040 seconds),
\(3.3\times\) at \(10^5\), and \(2.4\times\) at \(10^6\) (0.031 against
0.077 seconds), both on one thread. This result depends on the compiled
Rust kernel (Section~\ref{sec:perfbackbone}). Without it, on release 1.39.0,
\pkg{fixest} was \(1.6\times\) faster at \(10^6\), and it remains the
reference to beat for users on a platform with no wheel.
\item \textbf{Callaway--Sant'Anna.} \statspai{} is faster at every size,
by \(1.2\times\) at 5{,}000 observations and \(2.3\times\) at
125{,}000 (0.035 against 0.081 seconds). The reference version
matters: under \pkg{did} 2.3.0 the same call took 0.457 seconds at the
largest size.
\item \textbf{Classical SCM.} On the common ADH task the two are level
at 20 and 50 donors (0.73 against 0.75 seconds, and 1.20 against 1.29)
and \statspai{} is \(3.5\times\) faster at 100 (1.3 against 4.6
seconds). The outer search over \(V\) uses six starts, to detect non-unique solutions,
and each evaluation solves the inner weight problem directly when its
minimiser is certified unique. Before that solver, on release 1.34.0, the same task took 264
seconds at 100 donors and \pkg{Synth} was \(58\) times faster.
\item \textbf{DML PLR.} With linear learners \statspai{} is slightly
faster at every size (\(1.14\times\), \(1.14\times\) and \(1.09\times\);
0.0079 against 0.0087 seconds at \(N=10{,}000\)). The table calls any
ratio below \(1.5\times\) a tie. Nothing is claimed about flexible learners,
whose fitting time dominates both.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
No comparison favours the reference at the largest size, and DML is a
tie. The study supports a narrow claim. On the shared tasks measured
here, these entry points are no slower than their references. The overhead of
the unified interface was not measured separately. The study does not support a
general ``\proglang{Python} is faster'' claim. Three of the four ratios
moved by a factor of 3.7 to about 200 across releases, when one side
shipped a faster implementation (a compiled HDFE kernel, \pkg{did}
2.5, an exact SCM inner solver), so a reader should treat any such ratio
as dated.
