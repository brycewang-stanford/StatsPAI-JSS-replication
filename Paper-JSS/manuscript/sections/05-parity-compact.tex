\section{Numerical parity and statistical validity}\label{sec:parity}

The empirical claim of this paper is narrower than the package surface,
and the census behind it is reported once, here. \statspai{} registers
\RegistryTotal{} public callables: \RegistryCertified{} are
\code{certified}, \RegistryValidated{} \code{validated},
\RegistryApiStable{} \code{api\_stable}, and \RegistryExperimental{}
experimental. The two evidence tiers are earned in exactly two ways:
\code{certified} by a comparison with a named external reference on
identical bytes, \code{validated} by known-truth recovery or replication
of published numbers. They answer different questions and are never
summed in this paper, since a combined figure would let the second borrow
the first's authority. Coverage rows and documented disagreements (T4)
are recorded against a function but earn nothing on their own. Both
tiers and the parity grade are derived from one artifact, the committed
parity index, through a single grade-to-tier function, and the tier
additionally requires a stable API: \ParityCrossLanguage{} symbols have
cross-language evidence and \RegistryCertified{} are \code{certified}
because \ParityCrossNotCertifiedNames{} is still experimental. Every
\code{certified} symbol names its reference and tolerance (an \proglang{R}
or \proglang{Stata} module for all but a handful whose canonical
implementation is a \proglang{Python} package, enumerated in
\code{PYTHON\_REFERENCE\_ROWS}), and an audit in the archive fails on any
certified or validated symbol without an attached artifact.

The all-registered denominator dilutes coverage:
\ParityClassTotal{} of the \RegistryTotal{} callables are result or
exception classes that can never carry a grade and \ParityInfraTotal{}
are infrastructure (tables, plots, schemas, data loading). Against the
\ParityEstimatorTotal{} estimator callables that remain, cross-language
coverage is \ParityEstimatorCrossLanguage{}
(\ParityEstimatorCrossLanguagePct{}); \code{sp.parity\_summary()} returns
this split at call time. All \RegistryStableHandwritten{} hand-written
stable symbols carry parity, reference, or API-contract evidence; the
remaining \RegistryAutoUnbacked{} stable auto-registered symbols are
API-stable but not parity-backed, a denominator revisited in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

A label is only as good as the chain that attaches it to the evidence.
Figure~\ref{fig:evidence-chain} lays out that chain as the package
implements it; each link has its own check, and each check failed at
least once on this package before it existed. The rest of this section
reports the evidence and those checks in turn.

\subsection{Estimands, identification, and inference contracts}
\label{sec:statistical-contracts}

Numerical agreement does not establish identification: two programs can
compute the same quantity while both are inappropriate for a design that
violates exogeneity, parallel trends, continuity, or overlap. The
contracts below are conditions on the user's design and data, not
properties a successful function call can certify.

For the linear rows, write \(Y=X\beta+u\).  The OLS coefficient is always a
linear-projection parameter; interpreting it causally additionally requires
the relevant conditional-mean restriction.  With instruments \(Z\), 2SLS
solves the sample analogue of \(E[Z(Y-X\beta)]=0\); a LATE interpretation for
a binary treatment further requires relevance, independence, exclusion, and
monotonicity.  Absorbing fixed effects changes the projection space, not these
identification requirements.  Track~A pins HC1 or the named cluster; the
classical default is pinned separately on the original Card extract
(Section~\ref{sec:validation-tiers}).

For staggered adoption, the target primitive is

\[
  \operatorname{ATT}(g,t)
  = E\!\left[Y_t(1)-Y_t(0)\mid G=g\right],
\]

identified under no anticipation, a stated never-treated or not-yet-treated
comparison group, conditional parallel trends, overlap, and no interference.
Callaway--Sant'Anna aggregates these group--time effects; Sun--Abraham first
forms cohort-by-relative-time effects and then interaction weights.  A joint
pre-trend test is reported, but failure to reject is not proof of parallel
trends and its power must be considered.

The RD row targets the sharp/fuzzy cutoff effect (or kink contrast) under
continuity, local support, and no precise manipulation; its parity row
uses the native robust bias-corrected interval at the native CCT
MSE-optimal bandwidth (Section~\ref{sec:track-a}).

For RD density the null is equality of the one-sided density limits at
the cutoff, a design diagnostic rather than an effect estimate. Classical
SCM and SDID construct weighted untreated counterfactuals, so
pre-treatment fit and the stability of the untreated outcome process are
central, and their placebo distributions are design-based diagnostics,
not i.i.d. sampling distributions.

DML PLR uses an orthogonal, cross-fitted score for the partially linear model
\(Y=\theta D+g(X)+\varepsilon\), \(D=m(X)+v\).  A causal reading of
\(\theta\) requires the corresponding conditional exogeneity and overlap
conditions in addition to nuisance-rate conditions.  The forest row targets
\(\tau(x)=E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x]\); its ATE/ATT summaries use an AIPW score and
therefore require conditional exchangeability and overlap.  PSM targets ATT
by default and requires conditional exchangeability, common support, and a
credible propensity specification; balance and overlap diagnostics remain
part of the analysis even when the parity row passes.

\subsection{Track A: Numerical parity}\label{sec:track-a}

Track A uses three layers. First, estimators are tested against known
DGP truths whenever a truth exists. Second, \statspai{} and the
canonical \proglang{R} implementation read the same \code{\%.16g} CSV
bytes and write full-precision JSON. Third, when a directly comparable
\proglang{Stata} command exists, the Stata side reads the same CSV and
emits a sibling JSON artifact. The current R-joined harness has
\RParityModuleCount{} modules. Of those, \StataParityModuleCount{} carry a
canonical or audited Stata reference; each of the
\NoStataModuleCount{} without one records a measured, command-level
reason in \code{compare.py::STATA\_SKIP\_REASON}.
Rows whose defaults differ for a documented reason are labelled as
convention gaps rather than as ordinary passes.

\paragraph{Which implementation ran.}
A parity row is evidence about a \statspai{} algorithm only if the
\proglang{Python} side ran one. A call-trace audit
(\code{scripts/trace\_parity\_provenance.py}) runs each module under a
profiler that records every call from \statspai{} into a package other
than the numerical substrate and every subprocess it launches. The trace
is bound to what it describes: it stores the SHA-256 of the module
script, of every \statspai{} source file whose code ran while the module
estimated, and of the committed result, with the versions of the
packages it crossed into. A contract test fails if the registered
classification and the trace disagree, if any of those files has
changed since the trace, or if the trace meets a package outside the
reviewed lists (classified \code{unclassified}, never native); a
counterexample test edits one file on a module's estimation path, leaves
the module script untouched, and requires the trace to go stale. Of the
\RParityModuleCount{} modules, \ParityNativeModuleCount{} run native
code (\pkg{scikit-learn} appears only as a user-chosen nuisance learner)
and \ParityThirdPartyModuleCount{} wrap a third-party library---panel
fixed and random effects through \pkg{linearmodels}, ARIMA through
\pkg{statsmodels}, fixed-effects GLMs through \pkg{pyfixest}---and are
marked in Appendix~\ref{app:parity} as evidence about the wrapper's
conventions, not an independent algorithm. None calls the reference it
is compared against. Before 1.31 two did: the Honest-DiD modules passed
\code{backend="honestdid"}, which hands the computation to the
\proglang{R} package, and the RD headline used the official \pkg{rdrobust}
\proglang{Python} port. All three now run native code with evidence of
their own. The same trace, run over the twelve original-data modules of
Table~\ref{tab:headline-parity}, found one more: until 1.32.0 that
ledger's RD row still called the port, so its agreement with \proglang{R}
to \(3.5\times10^{-16}\) was the authors' code compared with itself. It
now runs the native selector and agrees to \(3\times10^{-14}\). Seven of
those twelve modules run native code throughout; the five NHEFS modules
fit their propensity or outcome regressions with \pkg{statsmodels}
(GLM and OLS) while the weighting, standardization, and inference are
\statspai{}'s, and their rows are marked; none calls a port or a
reference, and a contract test holds both ledgers to their traces. The native smoothness FLCI is compared with a documented
modification of \pkg{HonestDiD} whose simulated folded-normal quantile
is replaced by the exact one (the unmodified package sits
\(3\times10^{-4}\) away, which is that simulation error), agreeing to
\(1.4\times10^{-8}\) for \(M>0\); at \(M=0\), where the interval has a
closed form (the GLS extrapolation of the pre-period coefficients), the
native solver is within \(2\times10^{-9}\) of it and \pkg{HonestDiD}'s
cone solver \(5.7\times10^{-6}\). The native relative-magnitudes set, a
port of the Andrews--Roth--Pakes conditional test
\citep{andrews2023inference}, accepts the same points of
\pkg{HonestDiD}'s specified finite \(\theta\) grid on sixteen cases---a
statement about the grid, not the continuous set; its least-favourable
hybrid, whose first stage is simulated, agrees within one grid step, a
stochastic screen. The native RD selector returns \pkg{rdrobust}'s
bandwidth and robust estimate on the RDsenate bytes to
\(3\times10^{-13}\).

\subsection{Evidence tiers and hard cases}\label{sec:validation-tiers}

\paragraph{Evidence is attached to configurations and outputs.}
A tier is attached to a function, but every artifact exercises one
configuration---a method variant, an inference option, a data condition,
a code path---and compares some outputs but not others. A parity row that
pins the 2SLS coefficient and its classical, HC1, and CR1 standard errors
says nothing about the HC3 standard error of the same coefficient. For the
twelve suite estimators the package therefore records, per artifact, the
values it actually ran on every dimension that changes the computation
(enumerated, never a wildcard) and the outputs it compared: the point
estimate, the standard error, the coverage of the interval, or a
diagnostic. A dimension is ignored only for an output that provably does
not depend on it (the 2SLS point estimate does not depend on the
covariance estimator), and the reason is stored with the exemption.
\code{sp.validation\_scope(result)} reads the configuration of a fitted
object and reports, per output, the strongest matching evidence
(reference, seed equivalence, stochastic screen, coverage simulation,
disclosure, or none) and an overall status: \code{covered} when the point
estimate and its standard error both have a T1 or T2 row,
\code{estimate\_only} when only the point estimate does,
\code{stochastic\_only}, \code{disclosure\_only}, or \code{not\_covered}.
A dimension the fit did not record is listed as unchecked and never
matches, and a queried value outside a dimension's domain is refused.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Output-level scope of the Card IV fit with
HC3 errors. The Card reference test pins the coefficient and the
classical, HC1, and CR1 errors, so the estimate is covered and the HC3
standard error is not.},label={lst:scope},captionpos=b]
>>> fit = sp.iv("lwage ~ exper + expersq + black + south + smsa"
...             " + (educ ~ nearc4)", data=card, robust="hc3")
>>> scope = sp.validation_scope(fit)
>>> scope["status"], scope["outputs"]["se"]["status"]
('estimate_only', 'not_covered')
\end{lstlisting}

Building the map at this resolution found defects in the package and in
the map itself. \code{sp.iv(..., vce="cr2", cluster=...)} silently
returned CR1 standard errors: the option fell through a keyword catch-all
that the k-class fit ignored. It now reproduces \code{sp.ivreg}'s
covariance bit for bit or raises for a method that cannot compute it.
The fixed-effects member \code{sp.fast.feols} was pinned to \pkg{fixest}
with \code{ssc="fixest"}, but its default charged absorbed effects that are
nested in the cluster variable; a coverage row run through that entry
point (the previous row ran \code{sp.panel}) found its CR1 errors 8\%
above the Monte Carlo SD, with coverage 0.979. The default now follows
\pkg{fixest} (coverage 0.955, SE/SD 0.99). The map's LIML row cited a
module that runs \code{sp.liml}, a separate code path from
\code{sp.iv(method="liml")}; it now rests on a test that runs the latter
on the same bytes. \code{sp.sun\_abraham}'s default aggregate, the
equal-weighted average of post-treatment event-time effects, had no
reference row (module 05 pins the cohort-weighted aggregate); it now
matches the same linear combination of \pkg{fixest}'s coefficients to
\(8\times10^{-12}\). An earlier pass of the map found \code{sp.iv}'s
default classical covariance, printed in Listing~\ref{lst:card-output},
likewise unpinned; it is now held to \pkg{AER}\code{::ivreg} on the
original Card extract at \(8\times10^{-11}\). The map still has
limits: it covers the twelve suite estimators only, and dimensions that
no fitted object records (for example, the data condition) cannot be
checked from a result.

\paragraph{Causal forest: which Monte Carlo error?}
For a stochastic estimator two uncertainties must be kept apart:
sampling variation across datasets, which the reported standard error of
the ATE estimates, and the algorithm's own Monte Carlo variation across
seeds on \emph{fixed} data. ``Agreement within combined Monte Carlo
error'' is a statement about the second, so it cannot be tested by
dividing the gap between one \statspai{} fit and one \pkg{grf} fit by
their sampling standard errors --- which is how an earlier version of
this row was graded. The forest evidence now separates three tasks.
\emph{Truth recovery and inference} are Track~B's (the AIPW coverage row
below). \emph{Estimand agreement} holds by construction: both engines
report the doubly-robust AIPW average \citep{athey2019generalized}, and
given the same forest the AIPW operator agrees with \pkg{grf}'s to
machine precision. \emph{Algorithmic agreement} is tested by refitting
each engine under 50 seeds (20 at 8{,}000 trees) on fixed data, at
500, 2{,}000, and 8{,}000 trees, on two datasets. The margin was chosen
after inspecting the first, the reference-parity fixture, on an earlier
engine, so that fixture is development data; the second, the Track~A
module-13 design, and the spaced-seed rerun of both were run after the
margin was fixed. Seeds are spaced \(10^5\) apart on both
sides, because \pkg{grf} forests grown from consecutive seeds share most
of their random draws: a first run with consecutive \proglang{R} seeds
understated \pkg{grf}'s seed-to-seed spread several-fold and made
\statspai{}'s forest look several times noisier than it is.
Table~\ref{tab:forest-seed-mc} reports the result. At every tree count
on both datasets a two-one-sided test establishes equivalence within
\(\delta = 0.1\) sampling standard errors---a difference that moves a
95\% interval endpoint by at most 5\% of its half-width---with an
upper bound of at most 0.049; with Welch--Satterthwaite \(t\) critical
values in place of normal ones (20 seeds per side at 8{,}000 trees) the
bound is unchanged to the third decimal. The engines' seed-to-seed
standard deviations are of the same order but not claimed equal: their
ratio ranges from 0.79 to 1.48, and both fall with the number of trees,
from about 13--14\% of the sampling standard error at 500 trees to
6--7\% at the default 2{,}000. The claim is equivalence, not identity:
the engines are independent implementations, and at 8{,}000 trees, where
Monte Carlo error is smallest, their seed means differ by up to 0.019
sampling standard errors. The single-fit Track~A row is one draw from
each distribution, a stochastic screen rather than a parity fact.
Artifacts: \path{tests/reference_parity/test_grf_seed_mc_equivalence.py}
and its fixtures.

\subsection{Track B: Monte Carlo coverage}\label{sec:track-b}

Track B checks 95\% interval coverage on known DGPs, and reports with
each rate what is needed to explain it: the estimator's bias, its Monte
Carlo SD, the mean reported standard error, their ratio, and the
interval length (Table~\ref{tab:track-b-b1000}). The artifact holds
thirteen \(B=1{,}000\) rows. Nine correspond to suite members---OLS,
2SLS, \code{sp.fast.feols}, Callaway--Sant'Anna, Sun--Abraham, sharp RD,
SDID, DML PLR, and the causal forest---and each runs through the member's
own entry point or one asserted bit-identical to it (\code{sp.ivreg} for
\code{sp.iv}; \code{sp.causal\_question} for the forest, whose estimate is
\code{cf.average\_treatment\_effect()} exactly). The other four are
supplemental: 2x2 DiD, entropy balancing, the interactive DML model, and
fixed effects through \code{sp.panel}, which is family-level evidence and
not credited to \code{sp.fast.feols}. Three suite members have no
coverage row by construction: the RD density test is a diagnostic,
classical SCM has no analytic interval, and PSM's inference evidence is
the Abadie--Imbens SE parity row.

Ten of the thirteen rates lie in the binomial acceptance region
\([0.931,0.967]\) of the table caption, and in all ten the mean standard
error is within 4\% of the Monte Carlo SD. Three fall outside it: SDID
just below (0.928), the interactive DML model just above (0.968; ratio
1.04, mildly conservative), and DML PLR well below (0.883). Sharp RD
(0.934) is inside the region but at its lower edge and over-rejects at
the null (below), so it is examined with SDID. These rows are explained
rather than graded. For \emph{sharp RD} and \emph{SDID} the mean
standard error is within 2\% of the Monte Carlo SD, so the shortfall is
not one of average scale; the tails of the studentized statistic, the
randomness of the standard error and its dependence on the estimate can
still lower coverage, and the ratio alone does not say which. For RD the
question can be settled directly: on the first 200 draws \proglang{R}
\pkg{rdrobust}~4.0.0 returns the same intervals as \statspai{} to
\(6\times10^{-12}\) and covers in exactly the same draws
(\path{tests/coverage_monte_carlo/mechanisms/rd_drawwise_reference.py}),
so the shortfall belongs to the procedure on this design, not to the
port. \emph{DML PLR} under-covers clearly (0.883), and two things
contribute: its bias is half a Monte Carlo SD, and its mean standard
error is 12\% below the Monte Carlo SD (ratio 0.88). \code{sp.dml}
returns \pkg{DoubleML}'s numbers to \(10^{-16}\) on the same folds and
learners, which rules out a porting difference; it does not make the
interval adequate, and the package says so where a user will see it: the
default-learner PLR configuration is reported by
\code{sp.validation\_scope} as \code{stochastic\_only}, with this row and
its 0.883 attached, and the limitation is in \code{sp.dml}'s
documentation. Varying one thing at a time on the same design
(\path{tests/coverage_monte_carlo/mechanisms/dml_plr_learners.py},
\(B=500\)) locates it in the nuisance fit: with the true nuisance
functions coverage is 0.952 and the bias vanishes; with a random forest
or a spline Lasso it is 0.940 and 0.938; with the default gradient
boosting it is 0.892 at \(n=500\) and 0.936 at \(n=2{,}000\), where the
bias is still 0.41 SD. DML's orthogonal score makes nuisance error
second order, not zero, and the default learner's regularisation bias on
this design shrinks too slowly to be negligible at these sample
sizes. These experiments point to nuisance estimation as the main
source of both the bias and the small standard error; they do not show
that the default learner is inappropriate elsewhere. \emph{Entropy balancing} over-covered at 1.000 in the
previous revision because its standard error held the weights fixed and
was twice the Monte Carlo SD; with the M-estimation variance it is now
0.945 with a ratio of 0.97 (Section~\ref{sec:parity-gaps}).
The \emph{causal-forest} row, recomputed on the forest's own
doubly-robust average (Section~\ref{sec:attachment-audit}; it previously
measured a separate AIPW estimator and over-covered at 0.977), covers
0.959 with a ratio of 1.04 at the default 2{,}000 trees; a 300-draw check
at 300 trees gives 0.973
(\path{tests/coverage_monte_carlo/mechanisms/forest_trees.py}).

\subsection{Further checks}\label{sec:cross-estimator}\label{sec:high-n}

Estimators that should collapse to the same estimand on a given DGP are
held to each other: Callaway--Sant'Anna, Sun--Abraham, multi-period DiD,
and BJS imputation must agree on a homogeneous staggered design within a
multiple of \(\sqrt{\mathrm{SE}_1^2+\mathrm{SE}_2^2}\). Two estimators on
the same data are positively correlated, so this is a loose screen for
gross disagreement, not an equivalence test, but it catches a shared bug
that one-reference parity cannot. High-\(N\) fixtures tighten selected
known-truth checks to two standard errors at \(N=5{,}000\) and
\(8{,}000\); Honest-DiD is pinned to the closed-form breakdown value of
\citet{rambachan2023more}. Event-study \emph{paths} need a different
test, because implementations differ in what each half of a path is
differenced against \citep{roth2026interpreting};
\code{sp.event\_study\_convention()} records that choice per estimator.
Running it found that \code{sp.did\_imputation} documented Stata's
\code{pretrends(k)} but shipped leads equal to the symmetric benchmark
times \(N_0/N\) \citep{li2025benchmarking}, so a violated assumption read
as satisfied; the default now reproduces Stata's leads and their standard
errors to \(1.2\times10^{-12}\) and \(8\times10^{-13}\).

\subsection{Adjudicating a disagreement}\label{sec:adjudication}

A parity ledger is only as credible as its procedure for the rows that
do \emph{not} agree, so the procedure is fixed in advance and every
registered tolerance carries the outcome. A disagreement on identical
bytes is assumed to be a \statspai{} defect until the first diverging
intermediate quantity -- design matrix, weights, residuals, sandwich
meat -- has been located. If the divergence is a documented choice on
both sides (a degrees-of-freedom divisor, a small-sample cluster
correction, an analytic versus influence-function variance), the
default follows the method's own authors, the alternative is exposed as
a keyword, and the gap is registered with its mechanism and bounded by
the tolerance. If the reference itself is the problem or the solution
is not unique, the row is graded T4 and must carry independent
evidence (a second reference, an analytic identity, or a uniquely
identified DGP). If exact agreement is impossible by construction
(forest randomization, bootstrap), the row is graded T3 only if both
engines are refitted on fixed data under many seeds and pass an
equivalence test against a margin stated in sampling-SE units; a single
draw per side, a handful of seeds, or a Monte Carlo tolerance is
reported as a stochastic screen and earns no grade. A gap that
cannot be located is not called parity: it is graded ``unexplained''
in the tolerance audit and reported here as an open item. Two contract
tests enforce the procedure mechanically: every \proglang{R}-joined
standard-error row of every passing module must sit inside its
module's registered budget, and every Stata standard-error row that
does not must be registered with a reconstruction of the Stata number.
In release \StatsPAIVersion{} all \RParityModuleCount{} modules pass the
first test, and the second holds two entries, each reproduced from
\statspai{}'s own quantities: the group-average SE of Stata \code{csdid}
(the fixed-cohort-share aggregation of the joint influence functions,
\(6\times10^{-15}\); module 04) and the PLIV degrees-of-freedom factor
\(N/(N-K)\) of Stata's \code{ddml} (module 71). The Sun--Abraham row is the procedure's worked
example: it was carried as an open 0.7--2.2\% item across two releases
rather than absorbed into a wider tolerance, and the bisection that
closed it in 1.24.0 found a defect on the \statspai{} side
(Table~\ref{tab:correctness-history}).

\subsection{Auditing the attachment, not only the number}
\label{sec:attachment-audit}

A parity harness checks that two implementations produce the same
number. It does not check the claim that sits one level above: that the
function a reader calls is the function the harness ran. Track A modules
are written against one entry point, while a package of this size
exposes dispatchers, thin wrappers and convenience names that are
\emph{said} to reach the same estimator core. Every such name inherits a
grade it did not earn on its own, and inheritance is where an
unfalsifiable claim can hide -- the numbers in the ledger stay correct
while the set of functions they vouch for quietly grows.

Auditing those attachments in release 1.26.0 found that, of eleven
registered alias attachments, seven cited a module that never called the
aliased name. Each surviving attachment now names a test that runs the
alias against the canonical entry point on the module's own bytes. One
did not survive: \code{sp.wooldridge\_did}, credited with the ETWFE
module as an alias of \code{sp.etwfe}, is a different estimator (its ATT
is \(6.1\%\) away on that module's bytes) and was demoted to the grade its
own evidence supports. The configuration map of
Section~\ref{sec:validation-tiers} found two more: its LIML row cited a
module that runs \code{sp.liml}, not \code{sp.iv(method="liml")}, and
its 2SLS rows mixed tests of \code{sp.iv} and \code{sp.ivreg}; both are
now backed by tests that run the credited entry point. The failure mode
also reaches coverage rows: Track~B's causal-forest row ran through
\code{sp.causal\_question()}, whose forest design fitted the forest and
then reported a separate cross-fit AIPW estimator, so refitting with 30,
300, and 2{,}000 trees gave bit-identical results. It now reports the
forest's own doubly-robust average, identical to
\code{cf.average\_treatment\_effect()}, and the row was recomputed.

\subsection{Correctness history and residual risk}\label{sec:parity-gaps}

Every output-changing fix is pinned by a regression test and listed,
with its version, in Appendix~\ref{app:corrections}. Two entries explain
the design better than the rest. The 1.23.0 fix shows what a reference
comparison catches that unit tests do not: \code{sp.did(..., weights=)}
never forwarded the weights to a staggered branch and silently returned
the unweighted estimand, which only \code{did::att\_gt(weightsname=)}
on identical bytes exposed. The 1.25.0 fix shows the limit of a parity
ledger: the causal-forest aggregation was wrong for a
\emph{continuous} treatment, a path no Track~A or Track~B row
exercised, and it surfaced in a worked example. A ledger validates the
configurations it runs and is silent about the rest.

The current release controls that residual risk in three ways rather
than by pointing to the number of past fixes. First, the evidence for a
fitted configuration is queryable: for any fit of the core,
\code{sp.validation\_scope} reports which artifacts ran its variant,
inference option, and code path and which outputs they compared, and reports ``not covered'' rather
than inferring coverage from a neighbouring configuration
(Section~\ref{sec:validation-tiers}). Second, the release is frozen: the
paper describes tag \code{v\StatsPAIVersion{}}, the archive carries the
source snapshot and every artifact hash, and changes after the tag cannot
alter a number printed here. Third, every recorded artifact can be
rebuilt from its entry point (Table~\ref{tab:reproduction}).
