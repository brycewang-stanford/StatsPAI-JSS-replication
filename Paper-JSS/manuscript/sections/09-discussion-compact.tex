\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The article's claim is about what a validation label in a broad statistical
package can be made to mean. A label can be attached to the implementation that
ran, to the entry point that was tested, to the configuration and the output
that were compared, and to the checks that apply to the fit, and each attachment
can be verified. We have applied this to one package only. Nothing in the design
is specific to it, but whether it carries over at a reasonable cost elsewhere is
untested.

The main limitations follow. First, the reach of the checks is partial. The
call trace covers the parity modules, the configuration query covers 40 entry points, and neither sees the data. For the rest of the registry the evidence
notes name an artifact, not its configuration or the outputs it compared.
\RegistryAutoUnbacked{} stable auto-registered symbols are API-stable but not
parity-backed (\RegistryAutoUnbackedClasslike{} class-like,
\RegistryAutoUnbackedFunctionlike{} function-like), and the other
\RegistryOtherApiEvidence{} symbols of the \code{api\_stable} tier carry API,
convention, or supplemental evidence only. \ScopedLimitationCount{} certified or
validated functions list scoped limitations in their registry entries. Second,
one parity row remains a disclosed disagreement, classical SCM on the calibrated
Basque replica, where reference implementations choose different local optima
(Section~\ref{sec:scm-identification}). The forest is an equivalence claim, not
parity, and the \ParityThirdPartyModuleCount{} rows served by third-party
\proglang{Python} libraries validate wrappers, not native algorithms. Third, the
\pkg{fect} and \pkg{interflex} comparison holds at user-specified tuning values
(Section~\ref{sec:ex-fect}). Fourth, \proglang{Stata} reproduction requires a
separate licence, and \NoStataModuleCount{} modules carry no \proglang{Stata}
reference. Fifth, Bayesian, causal-discovery, graph-identification, and
behavioural-agent evaluations require different loss functions and are outside
this article. Sixth,
within DiD, \statspai{} implements the apparatus of the
\citet{baker2026difference} guide but not the efficient combination of comparison
groups and baseline periods \citep{chen2025efficient} or selection-based
pre-trend diagnostics \citep{ghanem2026selection}. A user who needs these, or who
works within one family and wants every option, should use the reference.

The unification itself has costs. A unified API
necessarily lags its upstream references. When \pkg{did} or
\pkg{fixest} ships a new option, \statspai{} users wait for the ported,
parity-tested version. The validation regime that makes the port
trustworthy is exactly what makes it slower to ship. A user who strays
from the validated core onto a long-tail helper is relying on
software-engineering guarantees, not on statistical validation, and must ask
for the tier to learn that. Keeping \RParityModuleCount{} modules pinned
against two external ecosystems is also ongoing work that a single-method
package does not carry.

Next, we aim to bound the remaining classical-SCM disagreement, to extend
\(B=1{,}000\) coverage beyond the headline rows, and to replace the DML default
learner (Section~\ref{sec:track-b}). We also plan scored behavioural benchmarks
of the agent interface.

A final limitation is institutional. A large surface is maintained by a small
team with one principal author, and the correction history above shows the
result. The package is MIT-licensed, and the parity harness is scripted and
environment-pinned, so a third party with the reference runtimes can re-execute
it. Those facts make the work inspectable. They do not reduce the dependence on
its maintainers.
