\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The article's claim is about what a validation label in a broad
statistical package can be made to mean. A label can be attached to the
implementation that ran, to the entry point that was tested, to the
configuration and the output that were compared, and to the checks that
apply to the fit, and each attachment can be verified. The same design would serve any package that publishes
evidence tiers.

The main limitations are as follows. First, \RegistryAutoUnbacked{} stable auto-registered symbols remain API-stable but not parity-backed
(\RegistryAutoUnbackedClasslike{} class-like, \RegistryAutoUnbackedFunctionlike{} function-like). The other \RegistryOtherApiEvidence{} symbols of the
\RegistryApiStable-symbol \code{api\_stable} tier carry API, unit-contract,
convention, or supplemental evidence only. The validated claim is
limited to certified/validated entries and to the named rows and modules
in the validation-suite evidence. Within that claim,
\ScopedLimitationCount{} certified or validated functions list scoped
limitations in their registry entries, each naming an unsupported variant
or a convention, and the validation-evidence audit in the archive
tabulates them with the function and its evidence grade.
Second, only one Track~A row remains a methodological/T4 disclosure. It
is classical SCM on the calibrated Basque replica, where reference
implementations choose measurably different local optima. On the
original \code{Synth::basque} bytes the same specification brings all
three implementations within \(4\times10^{-4}\)
(Section~\ref{sec:scm-identification}). The disclosure is therefore
about data-dependent non-uniqueness, not a standing numerical defect.
The causal forest is graded T3 by its seed study, and its single-draw
Track~A row is a stochastic screen. The two forest engines are
equivalent to within a tenth of a sampling standard error, with seed-to-seed spreads
of the same order. That is an equivalence claim, not parity. The
\ParityThirdPartyModuleCount{} Track~A rows served by third-party
\proglang{Python} libraries validate \statspai{}'s wrappers of those
libraries, not native algorithms. The configuration map behind
\code{sp.validation\_scope} covers the twelve suite estimators, and
within them only the dimensions a fitted object records. For the rest of
the registry, the evidence notes name the artifact but not its
configuration or the outputs it compared.
Third, the \pkg{fect} and \pkg{interflex} cross-validation selectors for
the factor number, penalty, and kernel bandwidth were ported in 1.29.0,
but their folds are random. The three-ecosystem parity of
Section~\ref{sec:ex-fect} is therefore established at user-specified
tuning values. The selectors carry only given-fold score equality and a
stochastic screen of the selection, not an equivalence test. Fourth,
Stata reproduction requires a separate licence, so Stata rows are
migration bridges and not part of the licence-free core of the archive.
\NoStataModuleCount{} of the \RParityModuleCount{} modules carry no
Stata reference, each with a measured reason. Fifth, Bayesian, causal-discovery,
and behavioural-agent evaluations require different loss functions and
are outside this software-validation article. Sixth, within DiD, \statspai{} implements the apparatus of the
\citet{baker2026difference} practitioner's guide but not the efficient
combination of comparison groups and baseline periods
\citep{chen2025efficient} or selection-based pre-trend diagnostics
\citep{ghanem2026selection}.

The unification itself has costs. They are the price of the design, not
defects in it. A unified API
necessarily lags its upstream references. When \pkg{did} or
\pkg{fixest} ships a new option, \statspai{} users wait for the ported,
parity-tested version. The validation regime that makes the port
trustworthy is exactly what makes it slower to ship. The registry's own
census quantifies a second cost. \RegistryAutoUnbacked{} of the
registered symbols carry API-contract evidence only. A user who strays
from the validated core onto a long-tail helper is relying on
software-engineering guarantees, not on statistical validation, which is
why the tier is printed at call time.
Third, the evidence regime has its own maintenance bill. Most
correctness fixes in Table~\ref{tab:correctness-history} were found by a
parity or coverage artifact, and the rest by audits of how evidence
attaches to functions and options, or by a worked example. Keeping \RParityModuleCount{}
modules pinned against two external ecosystems is ongoing work that a
single-method package does not carry.
A fourth cost is a limit of the regime, not of the package. A parity
ledger validates the paths it exercises and is silent about the rest.
The 1.25.0 entry of Table~\ref{tab:correctness-history} concerns a
continuous-treatment forest path that no Track~A or Track~B row
reaches, and it surfaced only in a worked example. Hence the registry
records \emph{which} artifact backs a label, \code{sp.validation\_scope}
reports the configuration an artifact exercised, and the worked examples
are executed end-to-end.

Next, we aim to resolve or further bound the remaining
classical-SCM reference-disagreement disclosure, and to extend
\(B=1{,}000\) coverage beyond the headline rows. Whether the DML default
learner should change is a question for more designs than the one
Track~B uses, and we will decide it on that evidence. We also plan to replace
the inner solver that dominates the synthetic-control gap of
Section~\ref{sec:performance}, and to move the agent interface to scored
behavioural benchmarks.

A final limitation is institutional: a large surface is maintained by a
small team with one principal author. Properties verifiable from the
archive bound this risk. The package is MIT-licensed
with no relicensing clause, so the validated core can be forked. Every
output-changing fix is pinned by a regression test. The cross-language
parity harness is fully scripted and environment-pinned
(\code{renv.lock}, recorded Stata versions, per-row JSON provenance), so
any third party with the reference runtimes can re-execute it.
